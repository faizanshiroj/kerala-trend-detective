How to read this

  • Mann-Kendall test checks whether values rise or fall consistently over the years. If p < 0.05 we call the trend significant, meaning it is unlikely to be random year-to-year variation.
  • Sen’s slope is the size of the change per year. The total change is the slope multiplied by the years in the record.
  • Limits: NASA POWER is a coarse grid (about 50 km), so neighbouring districts share similar values. We ran 350 tests, so around 5% of “significant” results (roughly 17) could be false alarms by chance. Treat single results with care and look for patterns across districts.
  • Data: NASA POWER monthly values from NASA satellite and model products (MERRA-2, GPM IMERG), accessed through the POWER API.
